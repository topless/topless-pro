# Sourcing atlas

Where policy-grade evidence about beach dress codes lives, country by country, and how far each
country can be published at `official`. Compiled from a research sweep on 2026-08-22 in which every
source below was opened and quoted; the verified records are in `data/`, and the evidence for each
is in the pull request that added it.

What counts: a `sourceUrl` is the rule or the report itself, and the labels say which. An
authority, a decree, signage or the operator's own policy carries `official`; a municipal tourism
page or several consistent independent reports carry `tolerated`; a directory entry, forum, review
or listicle carries `community-reported`. Map pins are never evidence. Hearsay is published as
hearsay, never promoted.

Reliability, per region: **strong** — a statutory designation with a published list; **medium** —
designations exist but must be found one authority at a time; **weak** — no designation mechanism,
custom only.

## Where to go next

Ranked by how much can be published at `official` per hour of research:

1. **Portugal** (strong) — Lei 53/2010 makes municipal assemblies designate naturist beaches and the
   FPN keeps the list with deliberation dates; most of the list is still unrecorded here.
2. **Australia** (strong) — NSW names designated beaches in statute and councils publish the rest;
   Victoria, Western Australia and South Australia have their own designations.
3. **France** (medium) — every official beach rests on an arrêté; the FFN and its regional councils
   list them, and communes increasingly publish the arrêtés. Dozens remain.
4. **Italy** (medium) — six regional laws let comuni designate stretches; FENAIT and ANITA keep the
   lists with the delibera references.
5. **Spain, Croatia, the Nordics, the Baltics** (medium) — municipal beach pages and operator house
   rules, found one authority at a time.
6. **Greece** (weak) — no designation mechanism; Law 1399/1983 covers only the shore in front of a
   licensed naturist centre. Expect `tolerated` from municipal tourism pages and directories, and
   name collisions between beaches called "Paradise".


## Tier 1 — Greece


### Greece — the national layer

**Reliability: weak.** The only statutory route is Law 1399/1983 'Ίδρυση και λειτουργία κέντρων παραθερισμού γυμνιστών' (FEK A 145/11.10.1983), implemented by ministerial decision 539851/260/1983 (FEK B 718/13.12.1983). It does not designate public beaches. It lets hotels, campsites and purpose-built facilities operate as 'κέντρα παραθερισμού γυμνιστών' under a special licence issued by a decision of the board of the Greek National Tourism Organisation (EOT), approved by the Minister, valid for two years and renewable, after the opinion of the competent prefectural and municipal/community council (art. 1-2). Art. 5 then exempts nudism 'στο χώρο των εγκαταστάσεων των κέντρων και το χώρο του αιγιαλού και της παραλίας που βρίσκεται μπρος από αυτές' from Penal Code art. 353. So a beach is lawfully naturist only as the shore in front of a licensed centre.

National federation: None. Greece is listed by INF-FNI as a correspondent country (GR-Hellas-Correspondent), not a member federation; — <https://inf-fni.org/about-inf-fni/>.

### Greece — Crete

**Reliability: weak.** National level: Law 1399/1983 (ΦΕΚ Α 145/11-10-1983, 'Ίδρυση και λειτουργία κέντρων παραθερισμού γυμνιστών') is the only statute that makes nudism lawful in a defined place. ### Greece — Cyclades

**Reliability: weak.** There is no decree, arrêté or bylaw type that makes a public beach officially naturist in Greece. The only legal route is Law 1399/1983: an EOT-licensed naturist holiday centre (hotel/camp) makes nudity on the seashore directly in front of it lawful (Art. 5). Municipalities have no statutory power to designate nude beaches; at most a municipal tourism page describes a beach as a place where nudism is permitted or customary (Antiparos does this), or a tourist-office map marks a beach as nudist (reported for Syros/Galissas and, decades ago, Santorini/Koloumbo). No government, regional, tourist-board or Blue Flag list of naturist or topless-tolerated beaches exists; Blue Flag (blueflag.gr) lists water quality/facilities only. ### Greece — Dodecanese

**Reliability: weak.** The only statute is Law 1399/1983 (ΦΕΚ Α 145/11-10-1983, 'Ρύθμιση θεμάτων γυμνισμού'). Art. 1: 'Επιτρέπεται η λειτουργία στον ελληνικό χώρο κέντρων παραθερισμού γυμνιστών.' Art. 2: the licence is a special ΕΟΤ (Greek National Tourism Organisation) licence, 'χορηγείται με απόφαση του Διοικητικού του Συμβουλίου' and 'εγκρινόμενη από τον Υπουργό', valid two years and renewable. Art. 5: nudism inside the centre and on the aigialos/beach in front of it 'δεν αποτελεί αδίκημα κατά τις διατάξεις του άρθρ. 353 του Ποινικού Κώδικα'. The law licenses hotels/campsites, not public beaches; there is no statutory procedure by which a municipality designates a public beach as naturist.

National federation: None – INF-FNI lists Greece (GR-Hellas) only as a Correspondent, not a member federation — <https://inf-fni.org/Home/Federations/Europe/GR-Hellas-Correspondent>.

### Greece — Ionian Islands

**Reliability: weak.** There is no Greek statute or decree type that designates a public beach as naturist or topless-tolerant. Law 1399/1983 (ΦΕΚ Α 145/11-10-1983) legalises nudism only inside licensed 'Κέντρα Παραθερισμού Γυμνιστών' (naturist holiday centres licensed by the Greek Tourism Organisation, EOT, under a ministerial implementing decision) and on the shore directly in front of them (art. 5). Outside that, the only route is a local-government act: a municipal council can characterise a beach or part of it as a nudist beach (this has happened for Faliraki/Rhodes 1980, Sarakiniko/Gavdos 2011 and Velanio/Skopelos 2012), but no Ionian municipality has done so and no published register of such decisions exists.

**Reliability: medium.** There is no legal act, decree type or issuer that designates a beach as naturist in Croatia.
